"Question:<br />
Which information and incentive problems occur in an ideal-typical corporation and how are they mitigated?"	In an ideal-typical corporation, quality uncertainty before entering the company and behavioral uncertainty toward new shareholders arise; they are mitigated by liability limitation and control systems for monitoring.
"Question:<br />
How high is secondary market liquidity for an ideal-typical corporation?"	There is high secondary market liquidity: In an ideal-typical corporation, there is weak asymmetric information distribution between subsequent acquirers and first acquirers and therefore hardly any quality uncertainty in this respect. In addition, disclosure measures further weaken the asymmetry of information distribution. A market for trading already issued securities on secondary markets would therefore emerge.
"Question:<br />
What is a central disadvantage of an ideal-typical partnership?"	High coordination effort between several shareholders.
"Question:<br />
How high is secondary market liquidity for an ideal-typical partnership?"	There is hardly any or only little secondary market liquidity: In the ideal-typical partnership, there is a strongly asymmetric information distribution between subsequent and first acquirers and thus considerable quality uncertainty. The market for trading already issued securities on secondary markets would therefore not emerge due to adverse selection.
"true or false:<br />

The coordination costs of the large number of shareholders would exceed the costs incurred by appointing professional management."
